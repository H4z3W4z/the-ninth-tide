# Rusty Lake + Lovecraft
## Research and design opportunities for an original puzzle game

Prepared for Mike Pearson and Kristi | September 6, 2026

**The opportunity is strong:** create an intimate, illustrated point-and-click mystery in which useful discoveries steadily expose a reality that humans were never equipped to understand. The distinctive feature should be what investigating does to the world.

Rusty Lake supplies a useful model for approachable interaction, unsettling everyday tasks and a continuing mystery. Lovecraft supplies structures for dangerous knowledge, impossible spaces, deep time and evidence that changes the investigator's understanding. Their combination can make puzzle solving itself the source of horror. This is a design conclusion from the research, not a prediction of commercial success.

Three findings matter most:

- **Preserve satisfying reasoning.** The player should understand why a solution worked, even when its larger implications are frightening.
- **Give the unfamiliar something familiar to invade.** A boarding room, observatory, kitchen or archive can imply a vast hidden world through a few precise changes.
- **Build a distinct identity early.** An original institution, visual language and supernatural rule will do more for this game than a large catalogue of borrowed monsters.

The current Rusty Lake benchmark includes **Servant of the Lake**, released August 13, 2026. Its housekeeping tasks lead into the Vanderboom family's alchemical activities. Some store description text still says "upcoming," but the release date, purchase availability and recent developer interview establish that it is released. [Servant of the Lake](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3636770/Servant_of_the_Lake/)

**Recommended first experiment:** a 20-30 minute, single-player pilot in a coastal survey station. Restoring its instruments reveals that the station is being observed by whatever it was built to measure. This is an original proposal developed from the findings, with a detailed example later in the brief.

**Scope and reading guide.** The first seven sections research the two influences and their relationship. The later sections propose creative directions and a practical first build. Rusty Lake coverage avoids major endings. The Lovecraft reading guide explicitly flags story revelations. Platform, budget and Kristi's preferred titles remain open; her enjoyment of the series does not establish which features she most values. This is source research, not a claim of completed hands-on playthroughs.

## 1. What Rusty Lake is actually doing

Rusty Lake is both an Amsterdam studio and the interconnected fictional world of its games. Its founders trace the universe to Cube Escape: The Lake and Seasons in April 2015. An early code connected the games and changed an ending, giving players evidence of a larger story. The founders identify Twin Peaks as an inspiration. [Creators interviewed by itch.io](https://itch.io/blog/23246/peering-into-the-rusty-lake-an-interview-with-the-series-creators)

The narrative combines detective investigation, individual lives and family histories. Cube Escape follows Dale Vandermeer's investigation into a woman's death; Roots makes a family tree the progression structure; Underground Blossom organizes Laura Vanderboom's memories and life around metro stations. These are different ways of making a continuing mystery playable. [Cube Escape Collection](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1292940/Cube_Escape_Collection/), [Roots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/532110/Rusty_Lake_Roots/), [Underground Blossom](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2291850/Underground_Blossom/)

The basic controls are economical: navigate a scene, inspect or manipulate objects, collect inventory items, and use a selected item on something in the environment. Paradise's official instructions describe precisely that vocabulary. Complexity can therefore reside in relationships between objects rather than complicated controls. [Paradise: controls](https://rustylake.itch.io/rusty-lake-paradise)

**Design interpretation: the central loop.** A player encounters a concrete obstacle, notices a discrepancy, collects or manipulates an object, infers a connection, acts, and then studies what changed. The strongest reward can be a new interpretation of something already seen. A familiar object becomes a clue, an instrument, or evidence of wrongdoing.

**Tone is part of the design.** In a Google Play interview, co-founder Maarten Looise describes a slow progression toward discomfort rather than an all-out horror experience. The desired response can include surprise, confusion and emotion; visuals, dialogue, sound and music work together. This is developer intent, not proof that every player reacts the same way. [Google Play co-founder interview](https://play.google.com/store/apps/editorial?hl=en_US&id=mc_orphan_games_editorialevergreen_indiedev_interviewseries_rustylake_fcp)

For Kristi's game, the working hypothesis should be that the pleasure of noticing and solving comes first. Quiet dread, odd behavior and occasional absurdity can accompany that pleasure. Her liking Rusty Lake alone gives us no basis to add pursuit sequences, reflex challenges or repeated death.

**Best study order for this project:** use a short Cube Escape chapter to examine interaction, Roots to examine narrative organization, Underground Blossom for pacing and personal stakes, and Servant for contemporary contextual puzzle design. Add The Past Within if playing together becomes a central goal. This order is for studying design, not avoiding every franchise spoiler.

A first project can choose one strong organizing idea: a survey ledger, a building plan, a collection of recordings or a calendar. The series demonstrates several possible structures; combining all of them would create unnecessary scope.

## 3. Puzzle craft, visual craft and the hidden workload

In August 2026, Robin Ras explained that Rusty Lake starts puzzle design with locations and plausible objects that support the story. Opening puzzles should be accessible, with difficulty developing through the game. The studio now tests and adjusts puzzles throughout production; he acknowledged that some older puzzles were frustrating because they received less testing. He also described early worldbuilding rules around memories, trauma and reincarnation, without every story detail being predetermined. [Ras interview, 80 Level](https://80.lv/articles/puzzles-lore-inspiration-get-glimpse-of-rusty-lake-development)

Looise separately described greater attention to usability and a dedicated group of nearly 50 playtesters at the time of his Google Play interview. The practical lesson is that an unusual fictional world still benefits from careful communication and observation of real players. [Google Play co-founder interview](https://play.google.com/store/apps/editorial?hl=en_US&id=mc_orphan_games_editorialevergreen_indiedev_interviewseries_rustylake_fcp)

The art should not be mistaken for effortless simplicity. Paradise credits handmade pastel backgrounds to Johan Scherft; Paradox also credits his paintings. Hotel assigns music to individual rooms, while Roots varies themes by level. Specific places therefore have specific visual and musical identities. [Paradise](https://rustylake.itch.io/rusty-lake-paradise), [Paradox](https://www.rustylake.com/room-escape-games/cube-escape-paradox.html?mobile-app=true&theme=wiki), [Hotel](https://store.steampowered.com/app/435120/Rusty_Lake_Hotel/), [Roots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/532110/Rusty_Lake_Roots/)

**Our interpretation:** controlled composition makes important objects readable. Limited movement can make a small animation unusually consequential. Repeating a scene with one meaningful alteration lets recognition do narrative work. These are useful design hypotheses to test, not claims that every series entry uses an identical technique.

Ron Gilbert's adventure-design essay argues for visible goals, understandable solutions, story advancement and avoiding missed-item dead ends. It also favors giving a stuck player another productive task. These are attributed design principles, not universal laws. [Why Adventure Games Suck](https://grumpygamer.com/why_adventure_games_suck/)

For our game, adopt five rules: demonstrate unusual behavior before testing it; make failure informative; retain essential evidence; offer graduated hints; and give each substantial solution a visible consequence. The supernatural may be incomprehensible in scale, but the next meaningful action needs to be discoverable.

**Production reality:** Ras reports that Servant took more than 2.5 years from idea to finished game. That mature franchise project is not a schedule estimate for a pilot. It is evidence that scene count alone understates writing, art, sound, puzzle and testing work. [Ras interview, 80 Level](https://80.lv/articles/puzzles-lore-inspiration-get-glimpse-of-rusty-lake-development)

## 4. What makes horror Lovecraftian

The most useful working definition is horror produced by discovering that reality is larger, stranger and less concerned with human needs than our explanations allow. Monsters, secret societies and forbidden books can express that idea. Their presence alone does not supply the underlying experience. This definition is a synthesis of the texts below, not a rigid boundary for the entire genre.

Lovecraft's Notes on Writing Weird Fiction emphasizes atmosphere, suggestion and convincing ordinary detail around an impossible phenomenon. He distinguishes the underlying abnormality, how it manifests, and its effects. He also recommends separating the actual sequence of events from the order in which a story reveals them. [Notes on Writing Weird Fiction](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/essays/nwwf.aspx)

That approach translates especially well to mysteries: the designer knows what happened; the player reconstructs it in a different order. An apparently minor discrepancy becomes significant when a second source confirms it.

| Theme | Evidence in the fiction | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dangerous understanding | Cthulhu connects separate records into an alarming picture. | Corroboration becomes an action with consequences. |
| Impossible space | Witch House makes room angles significant to other forms of movement. | Alter relationships between familiar spaces. |
| Deep time | Mountains reconstructs a civilization vastly older than human history. | Let a small artifact invalidate a comfortable chronology. |
| Unstable identity | Shadow out of Time connects memory, another intelligence and an alien archive. | Let evidence challenge who performed an earlier action. |
| Environmental intrusion | Colour changes the behavior of a farm and its household. | Repeated domestic routines become tests of reality. |

Sources: [Cthulhu](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/cc.aspx), [Witch House](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/dwh.aspx), [Mountains](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/mm.aspx), [Shadow out of Time](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/sot.aspx), [Colour](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/cs.aspx).

**The productive tension:** puzzle games reward mastery; cosmic horror undermines the assumption that knowledge gives control. Our solution is to separate scales. A player can reliably repair a machine, decipher a chart or rescue one person while discovering that the larger phenomenon remains beyond control.

This preserves agency and curiosity. If every discovery merely punishes investigation, there is little reason to keep solving. Give the player meaningful local achievements and increasingly disturbing implications. A small human success can coexist with a universe that remains indifferent.

## 5. Four stories to read first

**Spoiler notice:** these descriptions reveal premises and mechanisms. The Erich Zann entry includes a significant late-story revelation. The proposed game mechanics are our interpretations, not instructions found in the fiction.

### The Music of Erich Zann

A lodger hears an anxious musician whose performance seems connected to something beyond an upstairs window. The street itself later eludes the narrator's attempts to locate it. The story creates enormous implications from a small room, sound and a boundary. **Major revelation:** the window's view violates ordinary geography. [Read the story](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/mez.aspx)

**Use for the game:** a contained first episode, an unseen neighbor, a recurring sound pattern and a view that changes what the building can be. A sound puzzle should have an equivalent visual representation so hearing acuity is not the test.

### The Colour out of Space

A meteorite resists familiar classification. A farm's apparently abundant growth becomes inedible, and disturbances spread through its environment and household. The danger is expressed through observable changes rather than a conventional antagonist. [Read the story](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/cs.aspx)

**Use for the game:** a greenhouse, pantry, sink, laboratory and well can provide recurring interactions. Players compare samples or successive observations. The process may be legible even when the thing causing it has no adequate human explanation.

### The Dreams in the Witch House

A mathematics student investigates a room whose sloping surfaces suggest unusual spatial relationships. Historical accounts, geometry and dreams begin to illuminate one another. The built environment becomes part of the mystery's mechanism. [Read the story](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/dwh.aspx)

**Use for the game:** align a floor plan, shadow or model to connect rooms in an unexpected way. Introduce one transformation rule at a time and preserve an understandable navigation interface.

### The Call of Cthulhu

An inherited collection brings together sculpture, dream reports, press cuttings, police testimony and maritime evidence. Their relationship is more disturbing than any isolated clue. The opening explicitly makes the correlation of knowledge dangerous. [Read the story](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/cc.aspx)

**Use for the game:** give each source a partial, independently understandable observation. The player earns the revelation by matching dates, shapes, positions or testimony. The answer should reorganize evidence already encountered rather than arrive as a final explanatory speech.

**Suggested sequence:** Erich Zann for scope and atmosphere, Colour for environmental change, Witch House for spatial rules, then Cthulhu for the structure of investigation. These are the most directly useful starting points for the proposed format.

## 6. Four deeper references, and what to change

**Major story spoilers follow.** These are useful after the initial reading set, particularly if the game develops a larger history.

**The Case of Charles Dexter Ward:** historical research leads to an ancestor's experiments and a concealed laboratory. Catalogued materials acquire a horrifying significance as evidence of systematic exploitation of the dead. An inventory can therefore become a story: the player discovers what previously anonymous containers actually represent. [Read the story](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/cdw.aspx)

**At the Mountains of Madness:** murals, maps, architecture and biological evidence allow explorers to reconstruct a nonhuman civilization. Its history is encountered in fragments and out of order. For a game, repeated depictions with meaningful differences can teach the player how to reconstruct events and reassess apparently familiar figures. A museum room can suggest the same temporal scale as a vast expedition. [Read the story](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/mm.aspx)

**The Shadow out of Time:** experiences initially treated as dreams become connected to an alien archive and minds from different eras. The investigator's own participation becomes part of the evidence. This offers a model for records that challenge memory, authorship and identity without requiring a large cast. [Read the story](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/sot.aspx)

**The Shadow over Innsmouth:** civic secrets, local testimony and genealogical evidence ultimately implicate the investigator in the town's hidden history. It is useful for studying how public and personal records converge, but its ancestry-based horror also needs critical examination. [Read the story](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/soi.aspx)

### Adapt the sources deliberately

Lovecraft's racism is sometimes built into a story's explanation of what makes something frightening. Brown University's archival exhibition connects his correspondence and racial ideas to ancestry-based revelations in Innsmouth and Arthur Jermyn. Removing offensive vocabulary alone would leave those underlying assumptions intact. [Brown: Genotypic Horror](https://library.brown.edu/create/lovecraftracialimaginaries/genotypic-horror/)

Our adaptation can make inherited secrets about decisions, concealed crimes, promises, property or institutional obligations. Horror can arise from coercion, loss of agency and an indifferent reality. Neither mixed ancestry nor disability needs to function as evidence of evil.

Similarly, an optional perception mechanic should communicate altered access to evidence through consistent rules. Calling it "sanity" would not make arbitrary screen effects or withheld clues more meaningful. A protagonist can experience fear and disorientation while the player retains dependable controls and a useful record of observations.

## 7. Where the combination fits, and where it clashes

The pairing has a strong creative basis, but it is already part of an existing landscape. A Lovecraftian point-and-click game will need a more specific promise than its genre combination.

| Existing game | Verified emphasis | Implication for this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [The Last Door](https://thegamekitchen.com/thelastdoor/) | The studio explicitly cites Lovecraft and Poe; low-resolution visuals and an orchestral score support its mystery. | Suggestion and sound are viable references. The Lovecraft/adventure pairing already exists. |
| [Dreams in the Witch House](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1902850/Dreams_in_the_Witch_House/) | A direct adaptation combines point-and-click play with an open-world structure, time and character resources. | Resource pressure is a deliberate additional design commitment. |
| [The Room: Old Sins](https://www.fireproofgames.com/games/theroomoldsins) | An intricate dollhouse, concealed mechanisms and an unsettling disappearance story. | Study tactile object interaction as a separate dimension of puzzle pleasure. |

These product descriptions establish features and positioning. They do not establish the size of our audience, likely sales or unmet demand.

**The main tonal clash:** Rusty Lake's developers welcome ambiguity and gradual discomfort, while Lovecraft's craft essay insists on awe around the impossible phenomenon. Our choice should be to retain moments of ordinary warmth or dry humor, then give major discoveries space to land. Constant grotesquerie would leave little room for escalation. [Google Play interview](https://play.google.com/store/apps/editorial?hl=en_US&id=mc_orphan_games_editorialevergreen_indiedev_interviewseries_rustylake_fcp), [Lovecraft's craft essay](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/essays/nwwf.aspx)

**The main logic clash:** surreal fiction tolerates unresolved causes; a puzzle still needs an inferable solution. Keep essential operational clues in the game. Reserve unanswered questions for motives, history, scale or consequences. A player should be able to finish without reading a wiki or recognizing a mythological name.

**The main identity choice:** develop original people, places, art, sound, symbols and world rules. U.S. copyright distinguishes ideas and methods from protected expression. Duke's 2026 guide notes that works published in 1930 entered the U.S. public domain that year, but this does not clear every later Lovecraft text, edition, translation, illustration or adaptation. This is a general U.S. boundary, not a title or asset clearance. [U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html), [Duke: Public Domain Day 2026](https://web.law.duke.edu/cspd/publicdomainday/2026/)

My creative recommendation is an original mythology with Lovecraftian principles. It gives us control over both the mystery and its visual identity.

## 8. Three original directions

Everything in this section is a new creative proposal. Working titles are placeholders and have not been cleared for commercial use.

### The Ninth Tide

A temporary cataloguer arrives to close a remote coastal survey station. The previous observer has disappeared, leaving precise logs for tides that have not happened. Restoring the instruments causes undocumented parts of the building to become accessible. Eventually, the measurements begin describing the observer.

**Core activity:** compare logs, recordings and diagrams; restore apparatus; test a relationship; investigate the changed room. **Emotional center:** discover why the missing observer left a final record addressed personally to the protagonist. **Distinctive feature:** a completed observation changes the station's physical relationship to the outside world.

### The Still Harvest

A conservator inventories a shuttered estate's seed collection and fermentation rooms. Every vintage records a season absent from the estate's history. Restoring labels, testing sealed samples and comparing growth patterns reveals an agricultural process operating on a timescale the owners never understood.

**Core activity:** sort, sample, compare and reconstruct. **Emotional center:** an estate caretaker trying to preserve a livelihood while evidence makes continuation harder to justify. **Distinctive feature:** material transformation connects domestic work with environmental horror. This could take visual inspiration from wine country without making claims about a real winery or region.

### The Unlisted Room

A building custodian receives maintenance requests from a tenant whose apartment is missing from every floor plan. Small repairs change which rooms adjoin one another. The tenant's music keeps one connection closed, but an unanswered request is becoming increasingly urgent.

**Core activity:** repair fittings, compare plans and trace sound. **Emotional center:** deciding whether the unseen tenant is asking for rescue or containment. **Distinctive feature:** a compact building becomes a spatial mystery with a recurring relationship at its center.

**Recommendation: begin with The Ninth Tide.** It provides a coherent prop family, a credible reason to investigate, a bounded first location and an original relationship between evidence and world state. The Still Harvest offers the strongest domestic and ecological direction. The Unlisted Room offers the smallest dramatic footprint, though its music-and-lodging premise would need deliberate distance from Erich Zann.

These are alternatives to explore with Kristi. Her response to the central activity and mood should guide the choice before we write a larger lore bible.

## 9. A concrete pilot for The Ninth Tide

**Original design sketch; all counts and durations are targets.** One station room with four views, several object close-ups, five linked puzzles, one optional clue and a 20-30 minute first playthrough. The immediate goal is clear: restore the station's exit signal before the scheduled collection boat arrives. There is no real-time countdown.

The room contains a chart desk, a recording cabinet, a tide instrument and a locked observation hatch. A short duty card explains the ordinary job. The player can pursue chart and recording tasks in either order after an introductory repair.

| Puzzle | Evidence and action | Reward and unsettling implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Restore the desk lamp | A maintenance sketch shows the missing bridge; a spare is visibly labelled in the parts tray. | The lamp works. Its shadow reveals a pencilled note under a chart overlay. |
| 2. Reconstruct a recording | Matching notches and a labelled alignment mark order three recording strips. A written transcript mirrors the audio clue. | The recording supplies a calibration setting, followed by a log entry dated tomorrow. |
| 3. Reconcile the tide charts | Align two transparent charts using a shared fixed landmark and date marks. | A third curve becomes visible. It matches a scratch pattern already noticed on the hatch frame. |
| 4. Calibrate the instrument | Combine the recording's setting with the chart's marked interval; a practice mark demonstrates the relationship. | The hatch opens onto a dry stair where the exterior view showed open water. |
| 5. Complete the observation | A revised instruction is deduced from the station plan and newly visible stair marks. | The exit signal activates. A fresh record describes the protagonist's movements inside the room. |

**Fairness contract:** matching clues remain available; the lamp does not hide an essential item; failed configurations produce specific feedback; no correct solution consumes an irreplaceable item incorrectly. The notebook retains an observation even when the room changes.

**Ending:** the player accomplishes the immediate task and can leave the room. The larger revelation is that the station is an interface for reciprocal observation. The missing observer remains a meaningful question for a later chapter, while this episode still has a completed arc.

The pilot should establish a repeatable experience: familiar task, understandable deduction, useful result, disturbing reinterpretation. The twist belongs to the fictional world; the interface stays trustworthy.

## 10. Art, sound and rules that belong to this game

**Original direction for the recommended concept.** Use scientific illustration, worn survey diagrams, salt-stained timber, enamel labels and precisely drawn instruments. Favor ink blue, weathered cream and oxidized copper, with a restrained accent reserved for anomalous relationships. Keep interactive objects readable against less detailed backgrounds.

This gives the station its own visual vocabulary. Recurring marks should be designed as a learnable system: one shape for an observation point, another for a boundary, another for a relation. The player first sees each in an ordinary context and later understands its unsettling application.

**Animate consequences.** Spend motion on the moment a chart settles into place, a needle begins moving against its expected direction, or a distant reflection fails to follow the room. Give players time to notice the change. A static background still requires careful composition and continuity across states.

**Make sound informative.** Start with a stable acoustic identity: clockwork, wind, a loose fitting and occasional water. Alter one recognizable layer after important discoveries. For example, the outside water becomes silent while the instrument continues recording waves. Recordings can reveal evidence, but their informational content must also be available as text or visual patterns.

The fiction needs a short set of internal rules before additional rooms are written:

1. The station records relationships between locations, not only physical water levels.
2. A correctly completed comparison can make one previously hidden relationship accessible.
3. Every transformation leaves at least one stable trace so players can reason about it.
4. Observation works in both directions; the player gradually learns what the other observer can detect.

The precise nature of the other observer can remain uncertain. Its effects cannot be arbitrary whenever a puzzle needs an answer.

**Interaction requirements:** support click and tap, generous hit areas, readable item names, a visible selected item and an accessible observation log. Essential clues must not depend on color alone, precise pitch or rapid reactions. Hints should progress from a location nudge to the relevant relationship and only then an explicit action.

**Emotional requirement:** give the absent observer a recognizably human voice through a few economical details, such as an unfinished personal letter or a small repair made for someone else. The player needs a reason to care about the mystery beyond collecting explanations.

## 11. How to turn the research into a first build

Start by watching Kristi play one familiar favorite and one newer reference. Record which moments she enjoys: observation, inventory combinations, spatial puzzles, character revelations, atmosphere or shared deduction. Ask about her favorite title and preferred device while evaluating the concepts. These answers will materially affect the design; the research cannot substitute for them.

Then make the pilot in four stages:

1. **Paper version:** write the true event sequence and the player's discovery sequence. Draw the room and puzzle dependencies. Check that every required inference has available evidence.
2. **Playable rough version:** use temporary art and complete the whole loop, including inventory, notebook, hints and save/resume. Verify the story still makes sense when independent tasks are solved in either order.
3. **Finished sample:** complete one room's art and audio, then measure the actual effort. Use that evidence to estimate the rest of the project.
4. **Unprompted playtests:** include Kristi and several players unfamiliar with the design. Observe where they form the wrong hypothesis, overlook feedback or stop caring. Revise the evidence and presentation before adding rooms.

**Godot is a reasonable candidate**, especially if we want a reusable game project. For a browser pilot, current official documentation supports HTML5 export and identifies single-threaded export as the default. It also says Godot 4 C# projects cannot currently export to the web, and documents browser audio and persistence limitations. My provisional choice would be GDScript, early testing on the intended device, and a deliberately simple audio implementation. That is a design recommendation, not a benchmark of this unbuilt game. [Godot: Exporting for the Web](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)

A useful pilot passes practical checks: players know their initial goal; can explain why major solutions worked; notice the intended world changes; can resume with essential state intact; and want to investigate the next mystery. Completion time, hints used and comments should inform revisions rather than serve as arbitrary success scores.

The founders' historical free-to-premium path suggests that a complete sample can help demonstrate an unfamiliar world. It does not justify assuming the same commercial outcome today. [itch.io founder interview](https://itch.io/blog/23246/peering-into-the-rusty-lake-an-interview-with-the-series-creators)

**Research limits:** official descriptions are promotional; interviews report developer intent; the literary interpretations are reasoned readings. No market forecast, production budget, legal clearance or hands-on usability result is claimed. Research stopped when the core design claims had direct support and remaining questions required creative decisions or testing. The next useful artifact is the pilot's puzzle map and short world bible.
